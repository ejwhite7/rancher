# A fictional workflow-data assessment

A responsible workflow-data assessment connects a specific proposed use to evidence about rights, scope, preparation, quality and terms. The walkthrough below follows a fictional company and invented support records. It demonstrates decision points, including reasons to pause or stop; it is not a customer case study, a verified Rancher operating procedure or evidence of improved performance or licensing revenue.

## Set up the fictional case

Example Operations, an invented company, wants to explore whether a bounded history of support workflow events could support an external AI evaluation task. Its business sponsor authorizes a [metadata inventory](/blog/business-data-assessment/), not a transfer of records. The system owner is asked to describe event categories, approximate historical coverage, case identifiers and closure conventions. Message bodies, attachments and customer documents are outside the initial scope.

The proposed recipient has not accepted a dataset or committed to payment. Its stated interest is to evaluate how a system handles a sequence of support actions. That description gives the company a question to investigate: can it explain useful sequences within an appropriate boundary? The assessment does not begin with a claim that every support record qualifies or that the source history will necessarily meet the recipient’s needs.

## Build the metadata inventory

The system owner records that events can be associated with case references, that action categories changed during a process migration and that some closed cases later reopened. These are descriptions of the fictional setup, not measured findings from a real system. The inventory identifies the meanings that need clarification before any candidate dataset could be interpreted responsibly.

A field dictionary distinguishes event time from ingestion time and closure from verified resolution. It also identifies which relationships are explicit and which are currently uncertain. The team keeps evidence references and questions in the worksheet rather than copying actual records into it. The output is a description of what would need to be checked, together with owners who can explain or investigate the relevant source behavior.

## Fictional assessment decision trail

| Stage | Illustrative evidence or question | Possible decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inventory | Field dictionary and known migration gap | Describe scope without transferring records |
| Rights and exclusions | Unresolved origin for an acquired-system subset | Hold that subset outside the proposal |
| Preparation | Case tokens preserve joins but remove message context | Assess privacy and utility separately |
| Quality | Closure may be administrative rather than verified success | Revise labels or narrow the task |
| Sample gate | Specific recipient, purpose and handling conditions | Authorize a bounded step or decline |
| Commercial decision | Acceptance, costs and obligations remain unresolved | Continue, revise or stop without a revenue promise |

## Apply rights and exclusions gates

The legal and business owners identify the arrangements relevant to the proposed material and use. The team separates customer confidential content, workforce discussions, third-party attachments and credentials from the initial candidate scope. Where an agreement or origin cannot be established, the affected category remains unresolved. This is a decision process in the example, not a statement that a particular legal basis has been established.

The inventory reveals that a subset of historical records came from an acquired system with incomplete [documentation](/blog/ai-data-card-template/). The team excludes that subset while the responsible owner investigates. It does not treat the acquisition, system access or the age of the records as automatic clearance. The resulting proposal is narrower than the original history, and the team records the effect of that choice on coverage.

## Design a proposed prepared representation

For the remaining fictional scope, the team considers case tokens, event sequence, action categories and explained closure states. Exact identities, message bodies and attachments stay out. The team documents why each retained relationship is needed for the task and what privacy or confidentiality questions it raises. Stable tokens preserve joins but do not make the resulting records anonymous by themselves.

The before-and-after discussion identifies a trade-off: removing message content makes some action categories difficult to interpret. Rather than claiming the transformation solved every problem, the team asks whether a narrower evaluation task could use the remaining structure. If the necessary context cannot be included within an acceptable boundary, the proposal should stop or change. The example does not assume that every preparation problem has a satisfactory technical solution.

## Test interpretation and quality

The data owner proposes checks for missing case links, ambiguous timestamps, duplicate event representations and reopened cases. The [quality](/blog/ai-dataset-quality-checklist/) review also asks whether a closure label is strong enough for the intended evaluation. These are illustrative questions, not measured pass rates or universal acceptance thresholds. The recipient and company would need to agree the relevant requirements for the actual task.

A synthetic sequence, CASE-014, helps explain a failure mode. If a linked issue event is absent, a reader cannot infer the missing context merely from a later workaround. If the final closure is administrative, it should not be presented as verified success. The team documents these limitations and considers which cases would be excluded or labeled differently. It does not invent reasoning or outcomes to make the trajectory appear complete.

## Define any sample as a separate decision

Suppose the parties decide that a bounded sample could resolve a remaining question about interpretability. Before preparation, they define the fields, recipient, purpose, access conditions, retention and [permitted use](/glossary/permitted-use/) of that sample. The company confirms which decisions authorize the step and which remain unresolved for a larger delivery. Sample approval is not treated as permission for unrestricted access to the source systems.

The team also distinguishes evaluation from a possible later training request. If the recipient changes the intended activity, the affected scope and permissions need another review. The proposed sample does not establish a commercial commitment or demonstrate that the whole archive meets the same conditions. Its purpose is limited to the question the parties have agreed to investigate, with the result documented at the appropriate level of confidence.

## End with a go, revise or stop record

The fictional assessment ends with three possible routes. Continue only if the required evidence and acceptable terms support the next defined step. Revise if a narrower scope or task can address a documented limitation. Stop if rights, risk, utility, handling or economics cannot be resolved acceptably. The walkthrough deliberately does not assign a successful transaction as the inevitable outcome.

The worksheet records the decision, evidence references, open conditions and responsible owners. If the parties continue commercial discussions, they still need to agree deliverables, acceptance, fees and obligations. No invented customer quote, improvement metric or payment figure is used to make the example persuasive. Its value is the sequence of questions and the willingness to change course when the evidence does not support the original proposal.

## Before you move forward

- Keep every example explicitly fictional.
- Tie each next step to evidence and a responsible owner.
- Record narrower scopes and stop decisions as valid outcomes.
- Do not turn a sample discussion into unrestricted source access.

## Sources and scope

The ICO explains that pseudonymised personal data remains subject to data-protection law in the UK. NIST describes the AI Risk Management Framework as intended for voluntary use. These sources inform the questions above; they do not establish rights, safety, commercial value or acceptance for a particular dataset. The worksheet is a decision aid, not a clearance certificate.
